# Mars mission sources

The Phase 2 briefings are SIDERA VI educational simulations. Exploration radii and difficulty levels describe the simulated tasks. The coordinates are east-positive Martian regional reference points. The four USGS entries use planetocentric latitude and 0–360° east longitude. Columbia Hills uses the point listed in a JPL-hosted site worksheet. These points do not locate the cameras, define proposed EVA routes, or identify actual NASA crewed missions.

The JPEGs are available offline. The bundled copies retain the original image proportions and published colors. The iPad viewing panel crops to fit. The Jezero, Endeavour and Valles assets were downsampled for display.

## Jezero Crater · Moderate

- **Image** — Perseverance Mastcam-Z viewed the ancient delta from Octavia E. Butler Landing. Credit NASA/JPL-Caltech/ASU/MSSS. [NASA Photojournal PIA24485](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/perseverance-view-of-the-delta-in-jezero-crater/) · [Original JPEG](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/psd/photojournal/pia/pia24/pia24485/PIA24485.jpg)
- **Geography** — The crater center is 18.41° N, 77.69° E. Elevation data for the simulated survey area is pending. [USGS Gazetteer](https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov/Feature/14300) · [NASA Perseverance traverse map](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/location-map/)
- **Simulation** — A 1.0 km radius for an outcrop survey with several sediment observations along a rocky delta margin.

## Gale Crater · Easy

- **Image** — Curiosity Mastcam shows sandstone buttes on lower Mount Sharp. Credit NASA/JPL-Caltech/MSSS. [NASA Photojournal PIA21042](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/farewell-to-murray-buttes-image-2/) · [Original JPEG](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/psd/photojournal/pia/pia21/pia21042/PIA21042.jpg)
- **Geography** — The crater center is 5.44° S, 137.70° E. The approximately 5 km of relief describes Mount Sharp above the crater floor. [USGS Gazetteer](https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov/Feature/Gale) · [NASA/JPL Curiosity landing press kit](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/press_kits/MSLLanding.pdf)
- **Simulation** — A 0.5 km radius for a short observation loop that compares two sandstone outcrops.

## Endeavour Crater · Challenging

- **Image** — Opportunity Pancam captured the crater rim from Murray Ridge. The published mosaic is approximately true color. Credit NASA/JPL-Caltech/Cornell Univ./Arizona State Univ. [NASA Photojournal PIA18093](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/endeavour-crater-rim-from-murray-ridge-on-mars/) · [Original JPEG](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/psd/photojournal/pia/pia18/pia18093/PIA18093.jpg)
- **Geography** — The crater center is 2.28° S, 354.80° E. The 135 m measurement describes Cape Tribulation relative to the plain outside the crater. [USGS Gazetteer](https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov/Feature/14501) · [NASA Cape Tribulation summit view](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/opportunitys-view-from-atop-cape-tribulation/)
- **Simulation** — A 1.5 km radius for a rim traverse that compares ejecta with sand ripples and requires planning around loose sand.

## Columbia Hills · Hard

- **Image** — Spirit Pancam captured boulders on the hillside from near the base of the hills. Credit NASA/JPL/Cornell. [NASA Photojournal PIA06010](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/hillside-boulders/) · [Original JPEG](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/psd/photojournal/pia/pia06/pia06010/PIA06010.jpg)
- **Geography** — The site worksheet lists 14.5478° S, 175.6255° E in Gusev Crater. The approximate 90 m of relief describes Husband Hill from base to summit. [JPL-hosted site worksheet](https://marsnext.jpl.nasa.gov/documents/LandingSiteWorksheet_ColumbiaHillsGusev_final.pdf) · [NASA Husband Hill relief](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/the-columbia-hills-in-color/)
- **Simulation** — A 1.2 km radius for an ascent around loose debris to compare hillside geology with basalt from the plains.

## Valles Marineris · Extreme

- **Image** — The globe mosaic uses Viking Orbiter 1 imagery and shows the canyon system across the center. Credit NASA/JPL-Caltech. [NASA Science PIA04304](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/valles-marineris-the-grand-canyon-of-mars/) · [Original JPEG](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/psd/photojournal/pia/pia04/pia04304/PIA04304.jpg)
- **Geography** — The displayed center is 13.74° S, 300.80° E. The published maximum depth of about 9.3 km describes the canyon system as a whole. [USGS Gazetteer](https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov/Feature/6288) · [NASA canyon dimensions](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/valles-marineris-the-grand-canyon-of-mars/)
- **Simulation** — A 50.0 km planning radius for interpreting orbital imagery and finding a survey corridor among steep canyon walls and fractured terrain.
